The five checks

Five reasons a practice website never brings in a single client.

None of them are the design. Here's what each one actually costs you.

1. It doesn't say who it's for.

“Businesses of all shapes and sizes” tells a dental practice owner nothing. If a visitor can't see themselves on the page, the only thing left to compare you on is fee.

2. It's a brochure, not a shopfront.

Home. About. Services. Contact. That's a leaflet with a domain name. Nothing on it is trying to start a conversation with the person reading it.

3. There's no next step for someone who isn't ready today.

Most people reading it aren't switching accountants this week. They're switching in March, or after a bad VAT quarter. You offer them “call us” or nothing — so you get nothing, and you never learn they were there.

4. It lists services instead of problems.

Bookkeeping. VAT. Payroll. Year-end. That's a menu, and menus get price-compared. Name the problem you solve and the outcome you deliver, and price stops being the opening question.

5. It hasn't been touched in four years.

Broken on a phone, which is where the check actually happens. Team members who left in 2022. A copyright date that gives it away in the footer. None of that is a design opinion — it's a signal about how the practice is run.
